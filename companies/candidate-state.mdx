A candidate's stage is where they are in the job. There are six: **In review**, **In progress**, **Offer**, **Hired**, **Rejected** and **Withdrawn**.

| Stage | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **In review** | New in the job, promoted or applied. Nobody has messaged them yet. |
| **In progress** | Actively moving through your process. |
| **Offer** | You've extended, or are about to extend, an offer. |
| **Hired** | They accepted. |
| **Rejected** | Turned down. |
| **Withdrawn** | They pulled out. Only the candidate can set this stage. |

## Move a candidate to a different stage

You need the member role or higher in the workspace to change a stage.


From the **Candidate actions** menu, click **Move to stage…**. Pick **In review**, **In progress**, **Offer**, **Hired** or **Rejected**. Their current stage is greyed out. **Withdrawn** isn't in the list.

## Moving backward

You can move a candidate backward, for example from **Hired** back to **In review**, the same way you move them forward. On the [Activity tab](/companies/candidate-view), a backward move shows as a technical label, `state_corrected`, instead of a sentence.

## What happens to their process steps

Moving a candidate to **Offer**, **Hired**, **Rejected** or **Withdrawn** skips every process step they haven't finished. If you later move them back, those steps stay skipped. Turn each one back on with **Un-skip step** in that step's **Step actions** (…) menu.

## A stage change never emails anyone

Moving a candidate's stage never sends them a message. If you want them to hear about it, send a message yourself, or use the next action for that stage, such as **Send rejection note** or **Send the offer**. See [Next actions](/companies/recommended-actions).

It works the other way too. Sending any message to a candidate who's still **In review** moves them to **In progress**, whether or not you meant to change their stage.

## Move a candidate to a different job

**Move to job…**, also in the **Candidate actions** menu, lets you search your workspace's other jobs and move the candidate there. They leave the original job, so they can apply to it again later. You can't move a blocked candidate. You'll see "[name] is on the block list — unblock them first."

## Remove vs. Permanently remove

**Remove** takes a candidate off this job without telling them. They aren't blocked. You can add them again from a search, or they can apply again, but their earlier progress doesn't come back.

**Permanently remove** does the same and adds them to your workspace's block list. They can't be added to any job in your workspace, and future searches show them with a **Blocked** label.

<Warning>
The confirmation dialog says you can unblock them later, but there's no way to unblock someone in the app today. Use **Remove** if you might want this candidate back.
</Warning>

## Hiring doesn't close the job

Moving a candidate to **Hired** doesn't close the job. The listing stays open and other candidates keep moving through your process until you close the job. See [Close, reopen or delete a job](/companies/close-reopen-delete).

## Common questions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Can I undo a stage move?">
    Yes. Move them back with **Move to stage…**. Steps that were skipped stay skipped until you un-skip them.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Does moving someone to Rejected notify them?">
    No. Use the **Send rejection note** next action, or message them yourself.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can I mark a candidate as Withdrawn myself?">
    No. Only the candidate can withdraw their own application.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What happens to a scheduled interview if I reject someone?">
    Staffer skips the step, along with every other open step. If you move them back later, un-skip it from that step's **Step actions** menu.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can I undo Permanently remove?">
    No, not from the app today. Use Remove instead if you might want this candidate back.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
