# Staffer Copywriting

**The source of truth for how Staffer is explained, positioned and written.**

Use this document for website copy, feature pages, product copy, ads, emails, sales material, social posts, case studies and other reader-visible writing.

It combines the Staffer writing principles with the current `/v2` website copy and the product truths those pages establish. When writing new copy, preserve the product truth and the voice, but do not mechanically reuse old wording.

---

# 1. What Staffer is

## The core positioning

**Staffer is the AI hiring assistant that gets the work done.**

Staffer is an AI-native ATS for modern recruiting teams. It brings the hiring process into one place and takes on the work around every hire: finding candidates, screening applications, preparing outreach, scheduling interviews, following up and keeping the process moving.

The important distinction is simple:

**AI does the work. Humans do the hiring.**

That is a product philosophy and a trust layer. It should support the positioning, not replace it.

Staffer should not sound like another AI feature bolted onto an ATS. The point is that Staffer takes work off the recruiter's plate and turns the ATS from a place that stores hiring activity into a system that helps move hiring forward.

### The clearest explanation

> Your ATS stores the data. Staffer puts it to work.

A traditional ATS primarily records what the team has already done. Staffer uses the information already in the hiring process to help execute what needs to happen next.

### The simplest product story

**Understand → Find → Move → Decide**

- **Understand** — Staffer turns the brief into a clear hiring process.
- **Find** — It sources and identifies relevant candidates.
- **Move** — It screens, reaches out, schedules and follows up.
- **Decide** — It surfaces the right candidates and the evidence behind them.

The product should feel like one connected system rather than five unrelated AI features.

# 2. The central idea

## Recruiting is human. The work around it should not be.

Hiring still depends on human judgment, relationships and conversations.

But much of the work around those things is repetitive coordination:

- finding candidates
- reading applications
- updating records
- figuring out what happens next
- following up
- comparing calendars
- preparing outreach
- keeping candidates moving
- keeping the ATS accurate

Staffer takes on that work.

The recruiter keeps the context, judgment and final call.

This is not a disclaimer. It is the operating model.

---

# 3. What makes Staffer different

Do not try to differentiate Staffer with generic AI language.

The strongest distinction is **work**, not technology.

Most recruiting software helps people manage a process. Staffer is built to participate in that process.

### Old mental model

> Find the person → put them in the ATS → move the card → send the email → update the stage → book the interview → chase feedback.

### Staffer mental model

> Give Staffer the role and the context → Staffer works through the process → the team steps in where judgment is needed.

The copy should make this visible.

**Don't say:**
> Staffer uses AI to optimize your recruiting workflow.

**Say:**
> Staffer reads the pool, screens candidates against your criteria, finds the next step and prepares the work that needs your attention.

# 4. The writing philosophy

## Lead with the job, not the technology

Start with what the recruiter is actually forced to do.

Then show what Staffer takes over.

Bad:

> AI-powered recruiting automation for modern teams.

Better:

> Four hundred applications arrive. You've got time for sixty.

Best:

> Read the whole pool. Not every CV.

The reader should understand the problem before they need to understand the technology.

---

## Make AI operational

Never rely on words like:

- AI-powered
- intelligent
- smart
- agentic
- automated
- next-generation
- cutting-edge

unless the copy immediately explains what the system actually does.

Show the work:

> Staffer reads every applicant against the criteria in your brief.

> Staffer runs several searches in parallel.

> Staffer reads the calendars of every attendee and only offers times that work for everyone.

> Staffer builds a memory of your company, team and preferences.

> Staffer surfaces the next action from what has actually happened to the candidate.

The reader should be able to imagine the product doing the work.

# 6. Sentence and paragraph rules

- Prefer short sentences.
- Use contractions.
- Use active voice.
- Use concrete nouns and physical verbs.
- Write to "you".
- Use "we" when speaking for Staffer.
- Let important sentences stand alone.
- Use fragments intentionally.
- Make hard cuts between ideas.
- Avoid unnecessary transitions.
- Don't explain what the reader already understands.
- Don't repeat the same claim in three different ways.
- Don't inflate simple ideas with SaaS terminology.

### Rhythm

A good Staffer section often looks like:

> No email chains.  
> No comparing calendars.  
> No sending times back and forth.
>
> Staffer gives candidates the available times that work for the whole panel. They pick what works for them, and the interview is booked.

Short sentences create the tension. One longer sentence explains the mechanism.

# 7. The structure of strong Staffer copy

For a substantial page section:

### 1. Start inside their world

Give the reader a situation they recognize.

> Four hundred applications arrive. You've got time for sixty.

### 2. Name the real problem

Move the blame away from the recruiter's effort.

> The problem isn't that you can't physically skim a CV. It's that every candidate you don't have time to look at is a candidate you've chosen not to know.

### 3. Explain the mechanism

Show exactly what Staffer does.

> You write the criteria in plain language, mark which are required and which are preferred, and Staffer reads every applicant against them.

### 4. Show the payoff

Keep it concrete.

> Every applicant read end to end.

### 5. Give the reader a small next step

Prefer an action that lets them experience the product over a vague "learn more" or "book a demo".

> Screen a role you already closed.

# 8. One section, one idea

Do not put two competing headlines into one section.

Each section should have:

**One headline.**

Then:

**One clear idea.**

Then:

**The mechanism, proof or consequence.**

For example:

### Understands what you want

Staffer uses what it knows about your company, team and preferences to understand the nuance behind your brief, and find people who actually fit.

This is stronger than splitting the same idea into multiple overlapping headings.

# 11. The human-control model

The strongest way to communicate trust is to explain what Staffer does and where a person remains responsible.

### The core line

> Staffer handles the work. You make the decisions.

### The proof

- See why a candidate was shortlisted.
- See what Staffer looked at.
- See the evidence behind a score.
- See what needs your attention.
- Keep the context.
- Keep the judgment.
- Keep the final call.

For screening:

> A named human owns every candidate outcome.

For actioning:

> Recommendations, not decisions.

For sourcing:

> Staffer finds and ranks candidates. The team decides who moves forward.

# 12. The context advantage

This is one of Staffer's most important product ideas.

Staffer doesn't just process the words in a single prompt.

It builds a memory of:

- the company
- the team
- hiring preferences
- company facts
- role criteria
- feedback
- previous sourcing rounds

That context helps Staffer understand the nuance behind what the team is looking for.

### Core message

> Staffer uses what it knows about your company, team and preferences to understand the nuance behind your brief, and find people who actually fit.

This matters especially in sourcing.

Recruiting teams rarely want "a candidate who matches these keywords."

They want:

> Someone who fits what we're actually trying to build.

The copy should make Staffer's ability to learn those preferences tangible.

### Make the memory inspectable

Do not describe memory as magic.

The product lets the user open and correct what Staffer has learned:

> Everything it has learned is a row you can open and correct.

This is an important trust mechanism.

# 14. Actioning

## The idea

A pipeline tells you where candidates are.

**Actioning tells you what to do next.**

### Hero

> **Stop dragging cards**

> Every candidate already has a next step.

Staffer reads what actually happened: stage, activity, screening and elapsed time. It surfaces what needs to happen next.

### The problem

Traditional pipelines organize candidates around stages.

Recruiters still have to work out what to do.

That creates:

- stale stages
- forgotten follow-ups
- manual updates
- pipeline archaeology
- uncertainty about what matters today

### The mechanism

Staffer derives the next action from the state of the hiring process and groups candidates around those actions.

The queue is organized by:

- Complete step
- Send intro outreach
- Send follow-up
- Send offer
- Awaiting candidate

This is deliberately different from a traditional stage-based pipeline.

### Key copy

> A pipeline tells you where candidates are. Actioning tells you what to do next.

> Every candidate already has a next step.

> No cards to drag. No pipeline archaeology. Just the work that needs doing.

### Important truth

Actioning is recommendation-based.

Nothing in the Actioning queue sends itself or changes a stage without a person clicking.

That distinction matters.

# 15. Sourcing

## The idea

Finding candidates isn't just a search problem.

It's a **fit and context problem**.

### Hero

> **Find your dream candidate**

> Tell Staffer who you're looking for. It turns your brief into several searches, runs them in parallel, and brings back a screened shortlist.

### The core story

Staffer:

1. understands the brief
2. uses company, team and preference context
3. runs multiple searches
4. researches the market
5. finds relevant candidates
6. ranks them against the brief
7. learns from feedback
8. searches again
9. prepares contact details and personalized outreach

### The most important sourcing idea

> Staffer uses what it knows about your company, team and preferences to understand the nuance behind your brief, and find people who actually fit.

This is a key differentiator.

Avoid reducing sourcing to:

> AI finds candidates.

The important point is:

> It understands what you're actually looking for.

### Iterative sourcing

The shortlist should improve through conversation and feedback.

> Tell Staffer what was missing from the last batch. It searches again with your feedback in context.

> Keep steering until the shortlist looks right.

Each round remains visible so the user can see what earlier searches found.

### Sourcing to outreach

Sourcing shouldn't end with an export.

> Once you've found the right candidates, Staffer finds available contact details and prepares personalised outreach for each one.

> No export. No spreadsheet. Just the candidate, their next step and the work to move them forward.

### Important truth

Staffer does not send outreach on its own.

It prepares the work. A person reviews and sends it.

# 16. Screening

## The idea

The problem isn't that recruiters can't read CVs.

The problem is that they don't have time to read all of them.

### Hero

> **Read the whole pool. Not every CV.**

> Four hundred applications arrive. You've got time for sixty.

### The mechanism

The recruiter writes criteria in plain language and marks them:

- required
- preferred

Staffer reads every candidate against those criteria.

### Screening should be explained as evidence, not magic

Every candidate gets:

- a match score
- per-criterion scores
- evidence
- reasoning
- a state for each criterion

The four states are:

- Met
- Partial
- Gap
- Unknown

The **Unknown** state is important because it means Staffer couldn't establish something. It shouldn't silently turn missing information into a negative score.

### Key copy

> The score is a summary of that work, not a replacement for it.

> Open a criterion and you get the specific thing in their history that met it, and the sentence explaining the call.

### Screening is the engine, not the story

Screening becomes more valuable because the evidence feeds the rest of the process.

Inbound applicants and sourced candidates go through the same screening layer.

The screening result can influence:

- the next action
- process routing
- who gets scheduled
- who gets routed to a named person

### Trust

> We don't claim bias-free hiring, and you should be suspicious of anyone who does.

Then make the smaller, checkable claim:

> What we can do is make every scoring decision inspectable, keep the evidence attached to it, and keep a person's name on the outcome.

Never overclaim here.

# 17. Scheduling

## The idea

Interview scheduling is coordination work that shouldn't require a recruiter to become a human calendar algorithm.

### Hero

> **Automate interview scheduling**

> Staffer handles the interview coordination and keeps the hiring process moving.

### The experience

Connect calendars.

Set who needs to attend.

Let the candidate choose.

### Key copy

> No email chains. No comparing calendars. No sending times back and forth.

> Staffer gives candidates the available times that work for the whole panel. They pick what works for them, and the interview is booked.

### The mechanism

Each panellist releases the hours they're willing to give up.

Staffer intersects those windows with their real calendars.

Only slots that survive every attendee are offered to the candidate.

The candidate never sees the messy intersection.

That is the point.

### From interview to next step

The booking becomes part of the hiring record.

The activity is logged.

The process knows what comes next.

> The booking writes itself to the log with the actor and the time, and the next action is derived from the state of the process rather than typed in by whoever remembered.

### Notetaker

Staffer can join Google Meet as a notetaker.

It:

- records the conversation
- creates a timestamped transcript
- lets the team return to the conversation afterwards

Do not say it joins Zoom or Teams. It does not currently do so.

The recording and transcript are deleted six months after the application closes, and candidates are told on the invitation that the call is recorded.

# 18. Listing

## The idea

The job listing should be an extension of the company's brand, not another generic ATS form.

### Hero

> **Attract the best talent**

> Staffer turns your brief into a polished, branded job listing. Ready to publish, edit and share.

### Brand

> Set your brand once and Staffer carries it across your career page and every listing.

Listings can be edited in plain language and created in the languages the company hires in.

Translation covers:

- title
- tagline
- body
- apply button

The company name stays unchanged.

The primary language remains editable.

### Candidate application

> No long forms. No unnecessary questions.

A candidate drops in a CV and Staffer fills in the rest.

Current flow:

- name
- email
- message
- one optional field
- no account required

The candidate gets a private link to follow what happens next.

### Candidate portal

Every candidate has one place to:

- follow their application
- see what is next
- stay in touch with the team

The timeline updates as steps complete.

### Career page

One branded place for candidates to find every open role.

Publish a role and it appears on the page.

### Important truth

There is no native candidate mobile app today.

The candidate portal is a web page reached from an email link.

Do not imply an app exists.

# 20. The hiring process is one system

Avoid presenting Staffer as a bag of disconnected features.

The strongest product narrative is the connection between steps:

**Brief → Candidate → Evidence → Action → Interview → Decision**

For example:

A role starts with a brief.

Staffer uses that brief and the company's context to source candidates.

The same criteria screen sourced candidates and inbound applicants.

Screening produces evidence.

Evidence helps determine what should happen next.

The process schedules the interview.

The interview becomes part of the candidate record.

The next action is derived from the updated process state.

That continuity is one of Staffer's strongest stories.

# 21. The process builder

Staffer lets teams build the hiring process around the way they actually work.

### Core copy

> **Set your process on autopilot**

> Build the hiring process your way, then let Staffer run it.

Capabilities:

- define what happens after an application
- define what happens after an interview
- define what happens after a scorecard
- define what happens after a decision
- set custom stages
- set rules
- set actions
- create different processes for different roles and teams

### The mental model

A role's process is a canvas.

It can:

- start
- fork
- merge
- assign work to Staffer
- assign work to a named person

For example, applicants and sourced candidates can take different first steps before merging into the shared process.

### Critical behavior

If Staffer owns a step, it runs it.

If a step is assigned to a person, it stops and waits.

### Core line

> Never lose the thread. Staffer keeps track of what's happened, what's next and what needs a human.

# 23. Security and trust

Security copy should be factual and specific.

Current product claims include:

- EU data residency
- no training on customer data
- no-retention requirements on model requests
- encryption in transit and at rest
- named human on every decision
- append-only audit log
- GDPR processing under customer instructions
- DPA process
- published subprocessors
- retention and deletion controls

### Better than generic reassurance

Don't write:

> Enterprise-grade security you can trust.

Write the actual thing:

> Database, search index, files and the application itself are all in the EU.

Or:

> We don't train models at all, and every request we send carries a no-retention requirement.

Specificity is more credible than adjectives.

# 24. The ATS comparison

The comparison should never become a generic "legacy software bad" argument.

The distinction is operational.

| Work | Traditional ATS | Staffer |
|---|---|---|
| Finding people | Stores candidates you already found | Finds, scores, enriches and prepares candidates |
| Screening | Lists applications to review | Reads applicants against your criteria |
| Booking interviews | Records the interview after you book it | Finds a time that works for the panel and candidate |
| Follow-ups | Shows who went quiet | Prepares the follow-up and shows who is waiting on you |

The strongest line is:

> Your ATS stores the data. Staffer puts it to work.

# 25. Who Staffer is for

Staffer is built for recruiting teams that have enough hiring volume for coordination and admin to become a meaningful constraint.

A useful current qualification signal:

> If you hire fewer than about twenty people a year, Staffer probably isn't worth the switch yet. The admin we take off your plate isn't big enough for you to feel it.

This kind of concession is good Staffer copy.

It creates trust because Staffer is willing to say when the product isn't necessary.

# 28. Proof and honesty

The fastest way to lose trust is to claim something the product cannot demonstrate.

### Always

- describe actual product behavior
- distinguish recommendations from actions
- distinguish preparation from sending
- distinguish scoring from decision-making
- explain meaningful limitations
- use real numbers only
- use real customer quotes only
- date claims that can change
- keep product copy aligned with the current build

### Never

- invent a quote
- invent a customer
- invent a metric
- invent an integration
- imply a roadmap feature is live
- imply AI makes a final hiring decision
- claim bias-free hiring
- hide a material product limitation

A good Staffer claim should be checkable inside the product.

# 32. Microcopy

In-product copy is different from marketing copy.

Say:

- what happened
- what happens next
- what the user needs to do

No unnecessary exclamation marks.

# 33. Email

Cold email:

- under roughly 120 words
- open with their world
- no company introduction as the opener
- one idea
- one ask
- no feature dump
- specific reason to care now

Follow-up:

- one new idea
- no "just following up"
- don't repeat the first email
- make the next step easy

The best ask is often an experiment:

> Give us one real role and see what Staffer takes off your team's plate.

- personal experience

- community questions

But the underlying rules remain:

**Specific. Concrete. Human. No empty AI hype.**

A founder post should still say something real.

The following is the current reader-visible copy from the `/v2` landing page and six feature pages. Treat this section as the product-language reference. It is intentionally preserved so future copywriters can see the current narrative, terminology and level of specificity.

## Screening page

### Hero

> **Read the whole pool. Not every CV.**
>
> Four hundred applications arrive. You've got time for sixty. The problem isn't that you can't physically skim a CV, it's that every candidate you don't have time to look at is a candidate you've chosen not to know.

### Criteria, not keywords

> You write the criteria in plain language, mark which are required and which are preferred, and Staffer reads every applicant against them. The score is a summary of that work, not a replacement for it.

- **Per-criterion scores** — One number per criterion, not one number per person. A candidate strong on three things and weak on one is a different conversation from a candidate who is mediocre at everything, and a single score hides that.
- **Evidence and reasoning** — Open a criterion and you get the specific thing in their history that met it, and the sentence explaining the call. If the reasoning is thin you can see that it's thin.
- **Four states, not two** — Met, partial, gap, and unknown. The unknown is the one that matters: it's the system saying it looked and could not establish this, rather than quietly scoring it as a miss.
- **Scores that update** — New evidence changes the score. An AI interview, a take-home, a note from a screen. The record reflects what's known now rather than what was known on the day they applied.

### Testimonial

> "Staffer found our best engineer, and the marketer we only needed for 3 months"
>
> — Victor Helgeland, Founder, Marketer.com
>
> Every applicant read end to end. Your judgement gets to work on all of it rather than on the part you had time for.

### Screening is the engine, not the story

> A score that only ranks a list is a filter. The reason screening matters here is that everything downstream reads from it.

- **One layer, both sources** — Inbound applicants and sourced candidates go through the same screening against the same brief, so a 78 means the same thing whichever door they came in through.
- **It drives the next action** — The action Staffer proposes for a candidate is computed partly from their score. Strong ones get scheduled. Borderline ones get routed to the person you named.
- **It routes the process** — Score thresholds on a process step decide which path a candidate takes, so the shape of your funnel follows the evidence rather than the order things landed in the inbox.

### AI reads. A person decides.

> The more the AI does, the more it matters who is accountable. Screening is where that question gets asked first, so this is where we answer it.

- **A named human on every outcome** — Candidate decisions are assigned to a person and recorded with their name and a timestamp. Staffer never becomes the invisible decision-maker on a rejection.
- **The evidence is kept** — Per-criterion scores and the evidence behind them are stored on the candidate, so a decision can be explained months later without reconstructing it from memory.
- **Candidates are told** — AI involvement is disclosed to candidates. This is not a compliance checkbox for us. A process that has to be hidden from the person going through it is the wrong process.
- **EU data residency** — Candidate data is processed inside the EU, with a DPA, published subprocessors and retention and deletion controls. Built in Norway, hosted in Europe.

> We don't claim bias-free hiring, and you should be suspicious of anyone who does. What we can do is make every scoring decision inspectable, keep the evidence attached to it, and keep a person's name on the outcome. That's a smaller claim, and it's one you can check.
