A match score that just says "82" is a black box. Staffer shows the criteria behind the number, every time.

## Decomposed, not guessed

Each of a job's criteria is scored against the candidate individually, then combined, required criteria weighted higher than preferred ones. You can see exactly which parts of the fit are strong and which are stretch, not just a single blended figure.

## Explained in plain language

Alongside the number, the agent writes a short narrative on where a candidate is strong and where they fall short. It's commentary to help you read the score faster, not a black-box justification you have to trust blindly.

## The same scoring, reused everywhere

This is the same criterion-by-criterion scoring wherever a candidate is evaluated, in search results, on their profile, after an interview. One method, not three different scores that quietly disagree with each other.
